\begin{abstract}
This report studies the inverse action left unresolved by strong classical preconditioning, $d(r)=A^{-1}r-M^{-1}r$, for sparse polynomially augmented RBF--FD discretizations. Strong ILU already solves the tested Poisson, scalar-contrast, moderate node-irregularity, and scale-only systems efficiently. Rotated anisotropic diffusion creates an accurate but difficult regime in which system-own Ritz spaces can accelerate nonsymmetric recycled GMRES. Direct learned corrections did not outperform ILU. Learned subspace prediction became comparable with deterministic interpolation only within a narrow spatially constant coefficient family.

For spatially varying tensor fields, short Arnoldi constructions initially failed. Deeper trajectories and adequate retained dimension recovered an own-system union oracle on all 15 original hard fields, and rank adaptation was validated on 17 independently sampled hard systems. These successes did not yield reliable cross-field reuse. Training-dictionary truncation discarded solver-relevant information, while neural selection remained poor even when training targets were representable. Target-local probe construction recovered most teacher benefit on six of seven later hard targets once rank 48 was retained, but left a collective missing subspace on the remaining target.

Raw multi-RHS Krylov information contained a useful rank-12 correction for that failure. Its teacher-informed extraction reduced the difficult solve from the 300-step cap to 92 iterations. Neural mixing and a subsequently tested equivariant operator family failed to recover this effect. Direct projector analysis confirmed a large representation deficit in the tested operator span. The primary open problem is neural selection from a reusable dictionary using both the coefficient field and a very short target-local probe. The network must improve the probe-cost and solver-performance tradeoff relative to classical selection at the same probe depth.
\end{abstract}
